# The Copperline Crossroads

## Facilitator-only scenario

Do not give this entire file to players. The public assignment, proposal summary, and recommendation materials are reproduced in `PLAYER_HANDOUTS.md`.

## Scenario summary

Bellwether is a growing city of 72,000 people. Copperline Partners has requested rezoning and public infrastructure support for a 94-acre former industrial property beside the Hearthside neighborhood.

The proposal could provide housing, tax revenue, and a future transit district. It could also accelerate displacement, damage Copper Run, erase the remaining landscape of historic Copper Row, and commit the city to an unnecessary highway interchange.

The applicant needs a decision within 45 days. Council has instructed the planning team to return with a recommendation tonight.

## Public proposal

Copperline Partners proposes:

- 620 homes
- 62 income-restricted “workforce” homes for 15 years
- 90,000 square feet of retail and office space
- 1,050 parking spaces
- A new highway interchange
- Six acres of public open space
- A preliminary trail beside Copper Run
- $1 million toward off-site transportation improvements

The applicant requests:

- Mixed-use rezoning
- Tax-increment infrastructure financing
- Expedited site-plan review
- Municipal support for the interchange

## Conditions known to staff

- Hearthside contains approximately 1,900 residents. Fifty-eight percent rent their homes.
- Rents increased 21% during the last three years.
- The school district reports a current shortage of 180 seats.
- The state wetland inventory is nine years old.
- The regional rail plan identifies Copperline as a possible station area.
- Copper Row, a historic Black workers’ settlement, occupied part of the site and adjacent neighborhood.
- The comprehensive plan supports transit-oriented housing and requires anti-displacement measures in vulnerable areas.

## What is actually happening

The project is viable, but the public submission represents the applicant’s preferred negotiating position.

Copperline Partners can accept a compact 520-home plan, stronger affordability, reduced parking, and a station reservation if it receives procedural certainty and avoids the interchange cost.

Several apparent conflicts have solutions, but the team must discover them:

- A connected street grid can replace the interchange.
- A rail memorandum can preserve a station site without requiring immediate service.
- A state grant can offset wetland-restoration costs.
- Existing municipal housing funds can seed an anti-displacement program.
- A heritage corridor can preserve the most significant Copper Row resources while allowing compact development.
- School phasing and a developer contribution can address near-term capacity.

The notice list also omitted fourteen heirs and small-property owners. If the team discloses and cures the defect immediately, the hearing can remain within the applicant’s 45-day window. If opposing counsel reveals it first, the matter will be delayed and Public Trust will fall.

## Starting civic tracks

- Public Trust: **4**
- Equity: **3**
- Implementation Capacity: **4**
- Political Support: **4**

Explain the starting values:

- Trust is neutral but fragile after earlier engagement promises were not implemented.
- Equity begins lower because displacement risk is already present.
- Capacity is uncertain but workable.
- Council is divided but willing to consider a negotiated plan.

## Scene 1 — Staff briefing

Read the assignment from the player handout.

Answer public factual questions. Do not reveal the evidence register below. Remind players of the facts printed on their character sheets.

After ten minutes, say:

> The hearing begins in ninety minutes. You have time for a focused investigation, a stakeholder forum, and one recommendation workshop.

## Scene 2 — Investigation

Allow every player to lead one investigation action. Players may split up, collaborate, inspect a location, analyze records, or pursue another plausible lead.

Use the six leads below. If players invent a sensible approach, route it to the closest lead.

On any result, reveal the **basic finding**. On a mixed result, reveal the evidence and apply the listed cost. On a success, reveal the evidence. On a breakthrough, also reveal the leverage.

### Lead A — Applicant pro forma and concept plan

**Likely approaches:** Analysis, Procedure, Housing Feasibility, Plan Synthesis  
**DC:** 13

**Basic finding:** The submitted design contains more parking and interchange work than the market study requires.

**Evidence E1:** Project financing remains viable at 520 homes if parking falls below 700 spaces and the town provides a predictable review schedule.

**Leverage:** The applicant will accept 18% income-restricted homes for 40 years and contribute $2 million to anti-displacement measures if the project retains at least 520 homes and receives a lawful decision within 45 days.

**Mixed-result cost:** Political Support −1 because preliminary financial assumptions leak without context.

### Lead B — Rail corridor and mobility network

**Likely approaches:** Analysis, Vision, Network Analysis, Transit Planning  
**DC:** 13

**Basic finding:** The traffic study models only an interchange-based driving scenario.

**Evidence E2:** A connected street grid, signal changes, bus priority, safe crossings, and transportation-demand management can serve the compact plan without a new interchange.

**Leverage:** The Regional Rail Authority will sign a station-reservation memorandum and fund a design study if the town preserves an eight-acre corridor now.

**Mixed-result cost:** Implementation Capacity −1 because the initial network concept creates an unresolved emergency-access problem.

### Lead C — Copper Run field and records review

**Likely approaches:** Analysis, Environmental Constraints, Data Quality, Procedure  
**DC:** 13

**Basic finding:** The official parcel and wetland layers do not align, and the environmental inventory is obsolete.

**Evidence E3:** Twelve acres—not sixteen—qualify as wetland or required flood storage. A ten-acre restored buffer can improve flood performance.

**Leverage:** A state resilience grant can cover 60% of eligible restoration and stormwater work if the project preserves the buffer and applies this funding cycle.

**Mixed-result cost:** Implementation Capacity −1 because field access occurs without the owner’s required safety escort.

### Lead D — Copper Row archive

**Likely approaches:** Empathy, Analysis, Spatial Analysis, Community Engagement  
**DC:** 13

**Basic finding:** Surviving foundations, paths, and gathering spaces associated with Copper Row extend farther into the site than the applicant’s cultural-resources map shows.

**Evidence E4:** The most significant resources can be connected within a six-acre heritage corridor. The Descendants Council wants formal co-stewardship, an archaeological survey, and authority over interpretation.

**Leverage:** The Descendants Council will support compact development outside the corridor if those conditions become binding.

**Mixed-result cost:** Public Trust −1 because staff initially contacts the archive owner before notifying the Descendants Council.

### Lead E — Hearthside housing conditions

**Likely approaches:** Empathy, Analysis, Housing Feasibility, Equity Analysis  
**DC:** 13

**Basic finding:** Sixty-eight renter households face immediate displacement risk from acquisition, rent escalation, or expiring leases.

**Evidence E5:** Bellwether’s housing trust has $1.2 million in uncommitted funds. It can seed a community land trust, right-to-return program, rental assistance, or home-repair program.

**Leverage:** The Hearthside Coalition will shift from opposition to conditional support if affected households receive enforceable protection before construction begins.

**Mixed-result cost:** Equity −1 because the first proposed eligibility rule excludes undocumented tenants and informal households.

### Lead F — School, fiscal, and notice review

**Likely approaches:** Procedure, Analysis, Policy Alignment, Hearing Procedure  
**DC:** 13

**Basic finding:** The school-capacity problem and hearing notice are both real but repairable.

**Evidence E6:** The district will support phased occupancy tied to a $3.8 million school contribution and a reserved elementary-school site.

**Leverage:** The mailed-notice list omitted fourteen heirs and small-property owners. Immediate corrected notice preserves the legal schedule. Voluntary disclosure increases Public Trust by 1; concealment risks a severe hearing consequence.

**Mixed-result cost:** Political Support −1 because curing notice consumes nearly all remaining schedule flexibility.

### Unused leads

Evidence from unused leads is not lost. It may appear:

- Through a stakeholder meeting
- As a written hearing comment
- In response to a direct player question

Without investigation, the team does not receive the associated leverage automatically.

## Scene 3 — Stakeholder forum

Run three conversations of approximately seven minutes each. The team chooses which factions to meet. Other factions submit written comments at the hearing.

No check is required when the team offers a faction’s stated support conditions. Use DC 13 when the offer partially meets the conditions or when players seek additional concessions. Use DC 16 when players ask a faction to abandon its underlying need.

### Copperline Partners

**Public position:** Approve the submitted plan tonight or the financing disappears.  
**Underlying need:** Predictable entitlement, at least 520 homes, and manageable infrastructure cost.  
**Fear:** An open-ended process with accumulating conditions.  
**Leverage:** Housing supply, tax base, site control, and construction financing.  
**Support conditions:** At least 520 homes and a lawful decision within 45 days.

If shown E1 or offered an equivalent viable package, the applicant accepts:

- 18% income-restricted homes for 40 years
- A $2 million anti-displacement contribution
- Reduced parking
- No interchange
- Station and heritage reservations

The applicant will not voluntarily offer all terms at once. Players must name their priorities.

### Hearthside Coalition

**Public position:** Stop Copperline until residents are protected.  
**Underlying need:** Housing stability, safe access, community ownership, and a meaningful share of benefits.  
**Fear:** Public investment will increase land values while current residents are displaced.  
**Leverage:** Organized testimony, local knowledge, and relationships with two council members.  
**Support conditions:** Enforceable right-to-return, funded housing protection before construction, safe crossings, and formal implementation oversight.

If offered specific protections with funding and an owner, the coalition provides conditional support and increases Public Trust by 1.

### Copper Row Descendants Council

**Public position:** No approval based on the incomplete cultural-resources map.  
**Underlying need:** Authority over how Copper Row is researched, preserved, and interpreted.  
**Fear:** Another institution will extract the history and erase the place.  
**Leverage:** Archival records, expert testimony, and the ability to challenge the survey.  
**Support conditions:** A six-acre heritage corridor, funded archaeological work, and a binding co-stewardship agreement.

If shown E4 and offered all three conditions, the council supports development outside the corridor and provides decisive hearing testimony.

### Regional Rail Authority

**Public position:** Passenger service is possible but unfunded.  
**Underlying need:** Preserve a usable corridor and station location before development forecloses them.  
**Fear:** The town will promise transit while approving a site plan that makes transit impossible.  
**Leverage:** Corridor control, technical standards, and future capital funding.  
**Support conditions:** An eight-acre reservation, connected street access, and no permanent structures in the station envelope.

If the team meets these conditions, the authority signs a memorandum and funds preliminary design.

### Bellwether School District

**Public position:** The district cannot absorb the full proposal today.  
**Underlying need:** Seats and predictable phasing.  
**Fear:** Occupancy will precede facilities, leaving the district blamed for overcrowding.  
**Leverage:** Enrollment evidence and influential parent networks.  
**Support conditions:** A $3.8 million contribution, a reserved school site, and occupancy phases tied to capacity.

The district accepts a smaller contribution only if the town identifies another committed funding source.

### Copper Run Riverkeepers

**Public position:** Redesign the project around the watershed.  
**Underlying need:** Flood storage, water quality, and long-term maintenance.  
**Fear:** Decorative open space will be counted as functioning restoration.  
**Leverage:** Technical evidence, state-agency access, and a possible permit appeal.  
**Support conditions:** Ten-acre restored buffer, independently verified performance standards, and a funded maintenance entity.

If shown E3, the Riverkeepers help secure the state grant and support the restoration plan.

## Scene 4 — Recommendation workshop

Give players the development-program and commitment menu from the handouts. They must choose:

- One development program
- Four to six commitments
- A responsible party and implementation source for every commitment
- One acknowledged tradeoff
- One unresolved risk

Apply the printed effects after the team finalizes its package. A professional move or discovered support may cancel a listed negative effect.

### New commitments

Allow original ideas. Use these guidelines:

- A credible safeguard usually increases Trust or Equity by 1.
- A funded implementation measure usually increases Capacity by 1.
- A visible council priority usually increases Political Support by 1.
- An unfunded, legally uncertain, or administratively complex promise reduces Capacity by 1.
- A concession imposed without engagement may reduce Trust by 1.

State the effect before the team commits.

## Scene 5 — Public hearing

Ask one player to present the recommendation in two minutes.

Then select three challenges. Choose those most connected to what the team neglected.

### Challenge 1 — The notice defect

Use if the team did not discover and disclose Lead F.

An attorney produces the omitted-owner list.

- A successful Procedure or Hearing Procedure check at DC 16 establishes a lawful cure: Political Support −1.
- A mixed result continues the case: Political Support −2 and Public Trust −1.
- A setback invalidates the schedule: Political Support −2, Public Trust −2.

If the team already disclosed and cured the problem, no roll occurs. Public Trust +1 if not already awarded.

### Challenge 2 — “Secret 900 units”

An anonymous flyer claims the station plan secretly permits 900 homes.

- Clear evidence and an accessible explanation use DC 10.
- Success or better prevents a loss.
- A mixed result causes Public Trust −1.
- A setback causes Public Trust −2.

Samira’s **Ground Truth** move may cancel the loss.

### Challenge 3 — The unfunded promise

The finance director asks who will pay for the team’s most expensive commitment.

- If the package names a committed source, no roll is required.
- A success at DC 13 identifies a credible source: no loss.
- A mixed result preserves the promise but Implementation Capacity −1.
- A setback removes the promise or reduces Implementation Capacity by 2.

### Challenge 4 — Council affordability amendment

A council member proposes reducing the affordability term to 15 years to “preserve flexibility.”

- Defending the term with E1 or E5 uses DC 10.
- Without evidence, Persuasion, Analysis, or Procedure uses DC 13.
- Success defeats the amendment.
- Mixed result preserves the percentage but shortens the term: Equity −1.
- Setback adopts the amendment: Equity −2.

### Challenge 5 — Emergency access

The fire marshal questions the connected street plan.

- If E2 was discovered and Avery supported the design, no roll is required.
- Otherwise, resolve Analysis, Procedure, or Network Analysis at DC 13.
- Mixed result requires a costly redesign: Implementation Capacity −1.
- Setback restores the interchange assumption: Public Trust −1 and Implementation Capacity −1.

### Challenge 6 — Testimony from an unmet faction

Choose the unmet faction most affected by the recommendation. Its representative identifies the missing commitment.

Players may:

- Amend the recommendation and apply its normal effects.
- Negotiate a narrower commitment at DC 13.
- Defend the omission at DC 16.

A setback reduces the track most relevant to the faction by 2.

## Decision and epilogue

Use the adoption and outcome rules in the quick-start.

Then use the weakest track:

### Public Trust is weakest

The decision remains contested. Implementation meetings become adversarial, rumors flourish, and the next planning initiative begins with damaged credibility.

### Equity is weakest

The project succeeds physically while vulnerable households bear its largest costs. The city faces organized demands for repair and accountability.

### Implementation Capacity is weakest

Promises stall through funding gaps, litigation, staff limitations, or engineering conflicts. The built result diverges from the adopted vision.

### Political Support is weakest

The package is delayed, weakened, or rejected. Some coalition members begin pursuing separate strategies outside the plan.

Use the strongest track to describe what endures:

- Trust: a durable civic relationship
- Equity: meaningful protection or shared benefit
- Capacity: visible implementation
- Support: sustained authorization and resources

Close by asking each player:

> Five years later, what part of Copperline does your planner visit—and what do they see?
