# Design Brief

## Product

**Working title:** Plans & Parcels  
**First adventure:** The Copperline Crossroads  
**Prototype:** Version 0.1  
**Format:** Cooperative, facilitator-led tabletop role-playing game

## Experience statement

Players should feel like a capable but constrained planning team confronting a problem with no perfect answer. They should investigate conditions, hear legitimate competing interests, discover leverage, construct a defensible recommendation, and experience the consequences of their choices.

The session should produce professional recognition, civic drama, and restrained workplace satire.

## Primary audience

The first prototype is for planning professionals at an office gathering, professional association event, university studio, or informal game night. It assumes planning familiarity but no role-playing experience.

## Tone

- 60% civic problem-solving
- 25% professional satire
- 15% light fantasy

The setting is heightened and occasionally magical, but the central conflicts remain recognizable planning problems.

## Design pillars

### No perfect plan

Every major option carries costs. Success comes from understanding tradeoffs and building a durable coalition, not finding a hidden correct answer.

### Residents are not monsters

People and communities have interests, fears, histories, and internal disagreements. Antagonistic forces are scarcity, uncertainty, inequitable power, procedural failure, institutional inertia, and the occasional cursed spreadsheet.

### Every profession matters

Each character has information and capabilities that can change the final recommendation. No single role can solve the scenario alone.

### Failure moves the story

A failed roll creates a cost, delay, exposure, or hard choice. It never hides evidence required to continue.

### The recommendation is the climax

The final product is not a defeated opponent. It is a public recommendation that players must explain, defend, and live with.

## Core player activity

1. Interpret an assignment.
2. Investigate incomplete and conflicting evidence.
3. Build relationships with stakeholders.
4. Assemble a package of policies and projects.
5. Defend the package under public and political pressure.
6. Learn what happened after adoption.

## Shared measures

The party manages four civic tracks:

- **Public Trust:** legitimacy, transparency, and community confidence
- **Equity:** distribution of benefits, burdens, voice, and protection
- **Implementation Capacity:** legal, financial, administrative, and physical feasibility
- **Political Support:** likelihood of authorization, funding, and institutional backing

The tracks make tradeoffs visible. They are prompts for judgment, not substitutes for discussion.

## Minimum viable product

Version 0.1 includes:

- One complete rules engine
- One 90–120 minute scenario
- Six pre-generated planning characters
- Six stakeholder factions
- A structured investigation
- A recommendation-building worksheet
- A public hearing
- Multiple epilogues
- A playtest instrument

## Explicitly out of scope

- Campaign advancement
- Tactical combat
- A fantasy bestiary
- Detailed equipment
- Character creation
- Multiple municipalities
- Online automation
- Finished illustration or layout
- Commercial branding or licensing

## Success criteria

The prototype succeeds when:

- Rules take 15 minutes or less to explain.
- A session finishes within 120 minutes.
- Every player makes at least one consequential decision.
- No role consistently dominates play.
- At least 75% of players want another scenario.
- At least 75% can identify one planning insight from the session.
- Players describe the stakeholders as understandable, even when they disagree with them.

## Safety and representation

The facilitator opens with two boundaries:

1. Players may pause or redirect material without explanation.
2. Humor should target systems, professional habits, and fictional institutions—not marginalized communities or individual residents.

The fictional history of Copperline includes displacement and racial exclusion. Treat that history as a continuing planning responsibility, not decorative tragedy or a surprise twist.

## Version decision

The first test uses a familiar twenty-sided die, static difficulty levels, partial success, six fixed characters, and four shared tracks. These choices minimize explanation time and produce visible consequences.

The next design decision should be based on playtest evidence: either deepen this one-shot, simplify it, or begin a second scenario. Do not add campaign rules until two groups complete Version 0.1 successfully.
