# Quick-Start Rules

## What players do

Players portray a municipal planning team. The team investigates a proposal, speaks with stakeholders, creates a recommendation, and presents it at a public hearing.

The facilitator presents the world, portrays everyone outside the team, calls for checks when outcomes are uncertain, and applies consequences.

## What you need

- One twenty-sided die, called a `d20`, per player or one shared die
- One character per player
- The civic track sheet
- The recommendation worksheet
- Pencils and a timer

## Attributes

Each character has six attributes rated from 0 to 3.

| Attribute      | Use it to                                                  |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Analysis**   | Interpret data, test assumptions, identify patterns        |
| **Empathy**    | Understand experience, interests, fear, and harm           |
| **Persuasion** | Explain, negotiate, facilitate, and build support          |
| **Procedure**  | Navigate codes, law, hearings, and institutions            |
| **Vision**     | Design alternatives and connect actions to long-term goals |
| **Resilience** | Work through pressure, conflict, fatigue, and disruption   |

The approach determines the attribute. A player questioning a traffic model might use Analysis. A player explaining the same problem to an angry audience might use Persuasion or Empathy.

## Specialties

Each character has three specialties. Add `+2` when a check clearly falls within one of them.

Only one specialty applies to a check.

## When to roll

Roll only when:

- The outcome is uncertain.
- Success and failure would both change the situation.
- The action carries meaningful pressure or opposition.

Do not roll for ordinary professional knowledge. Do not roll when the team already has adequate evidence, time, authority, and agreement.

## Make a check

1. State the intended result.
2. The facilitator states the difficulty and likely consequence.
3. Choose the most relevant attribute.
4. Add `+2` if a specialty applies.
5. Add `+2` if one colleague provides meaningful support.
6. Roll `d20 + attribute + modifiers`.

Only one character may provide support. The supporting player must explain what they contribute and shares any consequence.

### Difficulty

| Difficulty             |  DC | Example                                                |
| ---------------------- | --: | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Routine under pressure |  10 | Locate a known record before a deadline                |
| Challenging            |  13 | Reconcile conflicting evidence or interests            |
| Exceptional            |  16 | Change a hardened position without meaningful leverage |

Most checks should use DC 13. Use DC 16 sparingly.

### Results

| Result                             | Outcome                                                                                              |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Meet or exceed the DC by 5 or more | **Breakthrough:** achieve the intent and gain an additional advantage                                |
| Meet or exceed the DC              | **Success:** achieve the intent                                                                      |
| Miss the DC by 1–4                 | **Mixed result:** achieve the essential intent, but accept a cost or reduced effect                  |
| Miss the DC by 5 or more           | **Setback:** the intended result is not achieved; reveal useful information and change the situation |

A natural 20 is always a breakthrough. A natural 1 is always a setback.

The facilitator may offer a hard bargain on a mixed result: the player can accept a stated cost or decline the result and try another approach.

### Appropriate costs

- Reduce one civic track by 1.
- Advance a deadline or trigger a hearing complication.
- Require a concession.
- Expose a conflict within a faction.
- Create a new implementation obligation.
- Force the team to choose between two opportunities.
- Limit the scope or durability of the result.

Failure never removes evidence required to finish the scenario.

## Professional moves

Every character has one professional move. Unless the move says otherwise, it can be used once during the session and does not require a roll.

Players should mark the move after using it.

## Civic tracks

The team manages four tracks from 0 to 8.

| Track                       | What it represents                                            |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Public Trust**            | Legitimacy, transparency, relationships, and confidence       |
| **Equity**                  | Fair distribution of benefits, burdens, protection, and voice |
| **Implementation Capacity** | Legal, financial, administrative, and physical feasibility    |
| **Political Support**       | Authorization, funding, and institutional backing             |

The Copperline scenario begins at:

- Public Trust: **4**
- Equity: **3**
- Implementation Capacity: **4**
- Political Support: **4**

Track values are public. Anyone may ask why a track changed.

After a consequential check, the facilitator normally changes no more than one track by one point. A recommendation choice may change several tracks when its listed effects say so. Values cannot fall below 0 or rise above 8.

Tracks describe the civic situation. They are not morality scores, and a high total does not erase a serious harm.

## Evidence

Evidence is a fact the team may use in negotiation or plan design. Record discovered evidence on the recommendation worksheet.

Some recommendation commitments have a cost unless the team discovers specified evidence or secures a stakeholder agreement. When that happens, mark the commitment as **supported** and ignore the listed cost.

Players may challenge inaccurate claims by citing evidence. The facilitator decides whether the evidence resolves the claim or creates a more favorable check.

## Scene procedure

During investigations and stakeholder meetings:

1. The facilitator frames the immediate situation.
2. Each player may take one leading action.
3. Other players may support, ask questions, or respond in character.
4. The facilitator records evidence, commitments, and track changes.
5. The scene ends when every player has had an opportunity to lead or the scene timer expires.

Do not require every conversation to become a check. A good question may simply receive an honest answer.

## Build the recommendation

The team uses the recommendation worksheet to establish:

- Proposed development program
- Four to six binding commitments
- Responsible party for each commitment
- Funding or implementation source
- One acknowledged tradeoff
- One unresolved risk

The recommendation may use listed commitments, modify them, or create new ones. The facilitator applies comparable track effects to new commitments.

A promise without a responsible party or implementation source is an aspiration. It does not increase a track.

## The public hearing

At the hearing:

1. One player presents the recommendation.
2. The facilitator introduces three challenges.
3. Each player may lead one response.
4. Previously discovered evidence and agreements remain valid.
5. Resolve checks and track effects normally.
6. Determine adoption and long-term outcome.

### Adoption

- Political Support 4–8: the recommendation is adopted.
- Political Support 2–3: the matter is continued or adopted with destabilizing conditions.
- Political Support 0–1: the recommendation is rejected.

Adoption is not the same as a good outcome.

### Long-term outcome

| Final condition                          | Outcome                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Total of 20 or more and no track below 3 | **Durable:** the plan survives implementation and retains a working coalition                                      |
| Total of 16–19 and no track below 2      | **Fragile:** the plan advances, but a major weakness shapes its legacy                                             |
| Any track at 0–1, or total below 16      | **Crisis:** rejection, litigation, displacement, fiscal failure, or institutional breakdown dominates the epilogue |

The facilitator should describe each track’s consequences. A single severe weakness must remain visible even when the overall outcome is durable.

## Player principles

- Ask what is missing.
- Distinguish a stakeholder’s stated position from its underlying need.
- Share the spotlight.
- Make commitments specific.
- Explain who benefits, who pays, and who bears risk.
- Treat uncertainty honestly.
- Remember that lawful, popular, equitable, and feasible are different questions.
